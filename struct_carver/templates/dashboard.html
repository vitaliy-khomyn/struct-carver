Forensic Evidentiary Standards & Interpretation Guidelines

Cautionary Notice for Forensic Examiners: Digital evidence reconstructed via non-sequential file carving requires critical corroboration. Automated classifications should never be treated as definitive legal conclusions without contextual investigation and manual validation.

Shannon Entropy Interpretation

  • Low (< 4.0): Indicates sparse, uniform data, zero-fill, or ASCII text. Inter-cluster null padding and gap zero-fills naturally depress entropy scores.
  • Moderate (4.0 – 7.2): Typical of structured documents, database pages, and uncompressed code/markup.
  • High (> 7.2): Indicates compressed archives (ZIP, GZ), media payloads (JPEG, MP3), or encrypted data. High entropy alone does not prove encryption, obfuscation, or malicious intent.

Payload Integrity Verification

  • VERIFIED: Confirms container syntax, chunk CRCs, or database PRAGMA integrity passed. It does not prove the file is free of malware, steganography, or malicious modification.
  • CORRUPTED: Indicates a checksum mismatch, truncated container, or invalid atom length. This often results from fragmented allocation or overwritten sectors, not necessarily intentional tampering.
  • N/A: Denotes formats without native checksums (plain text, RTF, BMP). Lack of automated validation does not indicate corruption.

Metadata & Timestamps

  • Extracted timestamps (EXIF, PDF dictionary, Office XML) reflect embedded application metadata, not filesystem MACB (Modified, Accessed, Changed, Birth) records.
  • Application timestamps can be forged, skewed by incorrect system clocks, or preserved from earlier template documents. Always cross-reference with filesystem journals and $MFT records.

Reconstruction Status & Slack Space

  • Slack space bytes represent unallocated remnants within the final physical disk cluster. They may contain residual historical data from prior deleted files.
  • Files marked Partial were truncated at gap-jump thresholds; intact initial fragments may still hold probative evidentiary value.
